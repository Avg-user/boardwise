# Alberta Employment Standards (summary)

Source: Government of Alberta, "Employment Standards Guide for Employees", January 2, 2026.
https://www.alberta.ca/employment-standards
If this summary and Alberta's Employment Standards legislation disagree, the legislation is correct. This is not a complete list of Alberta's employment standards.

## Minimum wage
- 18 and over: $15/hour
- Under 18 and a student: $13/hour (some exceptions apply)
- Under 18 and not a student: $15/hour

## Maximum hours per workday
- Generally no more than 12 consecutive hours, except for accidents or urgent work.
- Split shifts must fall within a 12-hour period (e.g. start at 9 a.m., finish by 9 p.m.).

## Breaks
- 5 hours or less: no break entitlement.
- More than 5 and less than 10 hours: at least one 30-minute break.
- 10 hours or more: at least two 30-minute breaks.
- A 30-minute break may be split into two 15-minute periods by agreement.
- Breaks may be paid or unpaid at the employer's discretion.
- If the employee cannot take a break, or cannot leave the premises during it, that time must be paid.
- Exceptions: accidents or urgent work, other unforeseeable or unpreventable circumstances, or different rules in a collective agreement.

## Pay periods and pay statements
- Pay periods must be regular and no longer than one month.
- Pay is due within 10 days of the end of each pay period, with a statement of earnings and deductions.

## Days of rest
- At least 1 day of rest per work week.
- Work weeks may be combined: 2 weeks → 2 consecutive days, 3 weeks → 3 days, 4 weeks → 4 days.
- At least 4 consecutive days of rest after every 24 consecutive work days.

## 3-hour minimum
If called in for a meeting or shift and sent home, the employee is paid the greater of their hourly wage × hours worked, or 3 hours at no less than minimum wage.

## General holiday pay
- Eligible after working for the same employer for at least 30 workdays in the 12 months before the holiday.
- Holiday on a regular workday:
  - Worked: hours × wage × 1.5 + average daily wage, OR hours × wage + a future day off at average daily wage.
  - Not worked: average daily wage.
- Holiday not on a regular workday:
  - Worked: hours × wage × 1.5.
  - Not worked: not eligible.
- Regular workday: a day the employee works consistently, or worked at least 5 times in the 9 weeks before the holiday.
- Average daily wage: total wages ÷ days worked over the 4 weeks before the holiday (or ending on the last day of the preceding pay period).
- General holidays: New Year's Day (Jan 1), Family Day (3rd Monday in Feb), Good Friday, Victoria Day (Monday before May 25), Canada Day (July 1, or July 2 if July 1 is a Sunday), Labour Day (1st Monday in Sept), Thanksgiving (2nd Monday in Oct), Remembrance Day (Nov 11), Christmas Day (Dec 25).
- Optional (if the employer recognizes them, the same rules apply): Easter Monday, Heritage Day (1st Monday in Aug), Boxing Day (Dec 26).

## Job-protected leaves
- Most leaves are available after 90 days with the same employer. They are unpaid; Employment Insurance may provide income support.
- The employee must give notice, preferably in writing. Some leaves need several weeks' notice or have extra requirements.
- Maximum lengths:
  - Maternity: 16 weeks
  - Parental: 62 weeks
  - Reservist: as needed
  - Compassionate care: 27 weeks
  - Bereavement: 3 days/year
  - Domestic violence: 10 days/year
  - Citizenship ceremony: half a day, once in a lifetime
  - Critical illness: 36 weeks (child), 16 weeks (adult)
  - Long-term illness and injury: 27 weeks/year
  - Personal and family responsibility: 5 days/year
  - Death or disappearance of a child: 52 or 104 weeks

## Vacation and vacation pay
- After 1 year: 2 weeks' paid vacation; vacation pay is 4% of annual wages.
- After 5 years: 3 weeks; vacation pay is 6%.
- Vacation pay may be paid on each paycheque or when vacation is taken.

## Youth employment
Additional rules apply to employees under 18: https://www.alberta.ca/youth-employment-laws

## Overtime
- Overtime is owed for hours over 8 per day or 44 per week, whichever total is greater; the employer must calculate both and pay the larger amount.
- Paid at least 1.5× the regular rate, unless there is a written banked overtime agreement.
- Agreements, written or verbal, not to pay overtime are not allowed.
- Banked overtime: at least 1 hour off (at regular wage) per overtime hour; it must be used within 6 months, after which it is paid out at 1.5×.
- Averaging arrangements change the calculation; the employer must give the employee a copy.
- Some roles are exempt (e.g. managers, supervisors, some salespersons): https://www.alberta.ca/overtime-hours-overtime-pay

## Ending employment
- Notice the employee must give when quitting (written):
  - 90 days or less: none
  - 91 days to under 2 years: 1 week
  - 2 years or more: 2 weeks
- Notice the employer must give (written):
  - Just cause: none
  - 90 days or less: none
  - 91 days to under 2 years: 1 week
  - 2 to under 4 years: 2 weeks
  - 4 to under 6 years: 4 weeks
  - 6 to under 8 years: 5 weeks
  - 8 to under 10 years: 6 weeks
  - 10 years or more: 8 weeks
- The employer cannot require the employee to use banked overtime, banked holidays or vacation during the notice period unless both agree.
- If told to leave immediately after notice, the employee must be paid for the full notice period.

## Deductions
- Required by law: income tax, CPP, EI, court-ordered garnishments or judgments.
- Also allowed: vacation pay paid before it was earned, and payroll errors from the last 6 months.
- Anything else needs advance written permission or a collective agreement.
- Not allowed: faulty work, uniforms, breakage, cash shortages when more than one person has access, and loss of property (e.g. dine-and-dash).

## Exceptions and variances
- Some industries have exceptions: https://www.alberta.ca/exceptions-for-specific-industries
- An employer with a government variance must give affected employees a copy.

## Complaints and help
- Raise the issue with the employer first (unless you have been fired, it isn't safe, or you fear reprisal); if unresolved, file a complaint: https://www.alberta.ca/file-employment-standards-complaint
- Employment Standards Contact Centre: https://www.alberta.ca/contact-employment-standards, toll-free 1-877-427-3731, Edmonton 780-427-3731.
